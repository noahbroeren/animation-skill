# title: Why Hot Air Balloons Only Fly at Sunrise
# style: toonworld-explainer
# topic: hot air balloons fly around sunrise (and near sunset) because the air is calm and stable before the sun heats the ground; daytime heating makes thermals and gusty, variable winds; balloons steer only by changing height into wind layers; pilots release small test balloons to read the wind; cold air gives more lift
A tourist paid for a balloon ride, and the pilot made him come at 4:30.
He wanted it warm and sunny, so he asked to fly at lunchtime instead.
@pilot: At lunch, this balloon goes wherever the sun wants.
So at noon, the pilot let go of a tiny test balloon, the kind pilots use to read the wind.
It shot straight up, dropped like a stone, then zigzagged sideways toward the power lines.
@tourist: Okay. That looked terrible.
The next morning they took off at sunrise, and the big balloon floated as smooth as glass.
The pilot never steered, because a balloon has no wheel and no rudder.
He just burned a little higher, and the balloon swung left, toward the river.
@tourist: Wait, how did we just turn?
Every layer of wind up there blew a different way, and he simply picked one.
Same valley, same balloon, so why was noon so wild?
You see, at noon the sun bakes the ground, and dark rocks heat up faster than grass.
Each hot spot shoots a bubble of warm air upward, and it scrambles those wind layers.
So what's different at 4:30?
Overnight the ground cools, so at sunrise the air lies flat and still, like a lake.
Cold air even gives it extra lift.
That's why in Cappadocia, about a hundred balloons lift off together at first light.
So when a pilot wakes you at 4:30, he isn't being mean. He's racing the sun.
