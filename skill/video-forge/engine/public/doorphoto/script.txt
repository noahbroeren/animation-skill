# title: Why Delivery Drivers Started Photographing Your Door
# style: toonworld-explainer
# topic: photo proof of delivery (Amazon drivers from about 2018, FedEx from 2022): "never arrived" refund claims, drivers blamed for missing boxes, the photo saved with the delivery time and GPS location
A man unboxed his 5th new phone that month, then swore it never came.
You see, back then the shop had nothing but a scan that said delivered, and his word against it.
So every time he said missing, the shop sighed and mailed him a brand-new one for free.
@boss: Refund him. Happy customers come back.
And he did come back, for a TV, a blender and a pair of golden headphones.
Then his neighbors heard about the trick, and soon half the street had boxes that never arrived.
Every missing box landed on the driver's record, one red mark at a time.
@driver: I left it on his mat. I swear.
Who wins, a tired driver or a customer with a sad voice? The customer, every single time.
By winter, the shop's pile of refunds stood taller than the delivery van.
Then one grey Monday, every scanner in the depot got a camera.
Why would a camera stop him?
You see, the scanner saves each photo with the exact time and the GPS spot where it was taken.
It's a receipt nobody can argue with: your door, your mat, your box, 2:14 p.m.
So the next time he called, the shop sent back a photo of the box on his own doormat, next to his own garden gnome.
And that's why your driver snaps your door before driving off: one photo, and no more free phones.
