# title: Why Delivery Drivers Started Photographing Your Door
# style: toonworld-explainer
# topic: photo proof of delivery (Amazon drivers from about 2018, FedEx from 2022): "never arrived" refund claims, drivers blamed for missing boxes, the photo saved with the delivery time and GPS location
A delivery driver parked his van at a blue door with his last box.
He set it on the doormat, tapped delivered on his scanner, and drove off.
Ten minutes later, the customer called the shop: the box never arrived.
@customer: Nothing on my mat. Nothing at all.
Back then, a delivery was just one tap, and the shop couldn't see a single doormat.
So it was the driver's word against the customer's, and the customer always won.
@boss: Refund him. Happy customers come back.
And he came back for a TV, a blender and golden headphones that never arrived either.
By Friday, his neighbors knew the trick, and half the street's boxes started vanishing.
Every missing box put a red mark next to the driver's name.
@driver: I left it on his mat. I swear.
By winter, the refund pile stood taller than his van.
Then one grey Monday, every scanner in the depot got a camera.
Why would a camera stop him?
You see, the scanner saves each photo with the exact time and the GPS spot where it was taken.
It's a receipt nobody can argue with: your door, your mat, your box, 2:14 p.m.
So the next time he called, the shop just sent one photo.
His box, on his *own* doormat, right next to his *own* garden gnome.
The driver's red marks vanished faster than any of those boxes.
And that's why your driver photographs your door before climbing back into his van.
