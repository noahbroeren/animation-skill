He made every manhole cover in town perfectly square. You see, the new mayor wants the streets to look modern, and square covers match the square tiles. The old foundry laughs at him. Square? Good luck with that. But square covers are cheaper, they stack neatly on a cart, and the mayor loves them. So by spring, half the town has square holes. Then one morning, a worker lifts a cover to check a pipe, turns it to set it down, and it slips. Straight down the hole. Wait, what? The cover is bigger than the hole. How did it fit? You see, a square is wider from corner to corner than it is from side to side. Turn the cover on its diagonal, and it slides right through. A circle is the same width whichever way you turn it, so a round cover can never fall into its own hole. By summer, the town quietly swaps every cover back. And the old foundry? They doubled their prices.
