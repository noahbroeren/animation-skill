# title: Why Square Manhole Covers Don't Work
# style: toonworld-explainer
# topic: why manhole covers are round (geometry: constant width)
A proud mayor orders every manhole cover in his brand-new town cast perfectly *square*.
He wants square lids to line up with the square paving stones around them.
[place:foundry] The old foundry, the only workshop in the valley that can cast iron covers, laughs at the order.
@founder: Square? Good luck with that.
[place:street] But the mayor points out that square covers are cheaper to cast and stack flat on a cart.
So the foundry pours them anyway, and by spring half the holes in town wear a square iron lid.
Then one rainy morning, a worker lifts a cover to clean the drain, turns it to set it down, and lets go.
The heavy lid tips onto its corner and drops straight down the hole with a splash.
Wait, what?
The cover was clearly bigger than the hole, so how could it possibly fall through?
Within a week two more lids disappear, and the town engineer starts measuring every one of them.
[beat:pivot] You see, a square is wider from corner to corner than from side to side, about *1.41* times wider.
Tip it up on its diagonal, and the long side slides right through the short gap.
A circle is the same width whichever way you turn it, so a round lid can never fall into its own hole.
By summer, the mayor quietly orders every square cover replaced with a round one.
[place:foundry] And the old foundry that laughed at the square order casts every round one, at double the price.
